# P110 — '70s–'80s pocket 110 cartridge · Calibration notes

**Reference material studied:** 110 cartridge scans (pocket camera family
archives, 110-revival community scans, lab samples of surviving cartridges).

## Observed characteristics

- **The tiny-negative look.** 13×17 mm negative enlarged like 35mm: soft
  detail, prominent grain, everything slightly mushy. Distinct from digital
  low-res — detail dissolves into grain rather than pixels.
- **Muted, aged color.** Low saturation, warm drift, lifted blacks — most
  surviving references are decades-old prints and it's part of the memory.
- **Motion softness.** Featherweight cameras + slow shutters: a whisper of
  blur on nearly every handheld frame.
- **Mild vignette,** soft corners from tiny fixed lenses.

## Parameter mapping (→ `FilmStockLibrary.p110`)

- Dissolving detail: `resolutionCrush 0.6` + `bloom 2.4` + zero sharpen —
  crush alone reads digital; the bloom melts it into grain
- Big soft grain: `intensity 0.20, size 0.95, chromaMix 0.22`
- Aged color: `saturation 0.82, blackLift 0.05`, warm temp 0.15
- Hand shake: bloom carries it at v1 (directional blur jitter noted for v2)

## Iteration log

- v1: crush 0.4 + sharpen mimicked DC-2000 — removed sharpen entirely and
  raised bloom; 110 softness is optical, never crispy.
- v3 (device test): every stock's grain size was ~3x too coarse — 2‰-class
  clumps render ~9 px wide on 12 MP and read as circular blobs, amplified
  by Lanczos-upscaled noise in the CI fallback. All sizes recalibrated to
  0.35–0.95‰ and the fallback now scales noise nearest-neighbor with a
  0.25x clump blur.
